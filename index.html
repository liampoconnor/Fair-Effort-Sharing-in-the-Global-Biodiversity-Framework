Fair effort sharing under the Global Biodiversity Framework

Prototype. Adjust the weighting on each fairness basis and read the resulting distribution of effort.

How to use this tool

The Kunming-Montreal Global Biodiversity Framework sets targets for the world to reduce its impact on nature, but it does not address how these costs should be shared fairly among countries. This tool divides figurative pools of effort needed to reach global targets between countries accordingly. As the user, you can choose whichever principles of fairness you think are most important when fairly dividing effort. The results help visualize what these choices could mean.

1Pick a target

Each of the three targets included in this model has a total to be divided:

money for total biodiversity finance
land and financing needed to reach 30% global protected area coverage by 2030
raw material use for sustainable consumption

The map takes on each target's color.

2Decide what a fair share should depend on

Each slider represents a fairness weighting for how one could divide effort. You can combine them or only apply one depending on what you think is fair.

  • Capacity — a country's economic capacity to pay.
  • Current impact — a country's impact on species loss today.
  • Cumulative impact — a country's impact on species loss since 1990.
  • Population — the number of people a country has.
  • Protection shortfall — how far a country is from protecting 30% of its own land.

The bar underneath shows the resulting mix from your choices, in these same colors.

3Additional fairness choices

  • Count overseas impact only — counts just the part of a country's footprint that falls beyond their own borders. This excludes the impact of a country's land use and internal consumption of its resources.
  • Apply development floor — reduces the capacity-based share for countries below a level of human development. This accounts for a country's need to invest in its own development.

4Read the result

  • Map — each country's share of the total. Hover to see its allocated figures.
  • Table — every country ranked, searchable, and downloadable as a spreadsheet.
  • Continuum — pick one country and watch its share move as the weighting shifts between two principles and compare to similar countries.

Important note: Fairness choices are already made implicitly in the process of determining national contributions, as well as during Convention on Biological Diversity deliberations. By developing this framework, we hope to provide a tool that makes these decisions explicit, and to show how different fairness assumptions translate into country-level responsibilities under the Global Biodiversity Framework.
